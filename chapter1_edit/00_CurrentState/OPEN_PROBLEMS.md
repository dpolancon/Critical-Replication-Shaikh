# Open Problems — Chapter 1 Editing System

## Main editorial problem

The manuscript is safer and more internally consistent than the earlier 2.0 version, but it has not yet been strongly transformed as a critical replication paper in applied econometrics / political economy.

## Current open problems

1. The introduction still sounds AI/system-like.
2. The introduction uses abstractions such as “measurement problem,” “replication target,” “interpretive move,” “candidate transformation elasticity,” and “system-admissibility gates” too early and too visibly.
3. The manuscript sometimes names empirical items as “objects” rather than directly naming coefficients, series, estimates, tests, specifications, measures, or benchmarks.
4. Section 3 remains conceptually dense and procedural at the point where it should make the coefficient’s theoretical role clear.
5. Section 2.2 contains strong political-economy material but may move too quickly from output-gap governance to shop-floor discipline, reserve army, and capital strikes.
6. Section 4 is claim-safe, but parts of its exposition still read like a technical protocol rather than a staged critical replication.
7. The previous pass architecture optimized internal consistency more successfully than genre fit.

## Current non-problems

1. The core econometric claim locks should be preserved.
2. The careful distinction between statistical retention and economic admissibility should be preserved.
3. The treatment of `\hat{\theta}=1.19` as statistically retained but economically rejected should be preserved.
4. Demand components should remain future research unless directly estimated.
5. No repo commit decision is needed before the next editing-system diagnosis.
