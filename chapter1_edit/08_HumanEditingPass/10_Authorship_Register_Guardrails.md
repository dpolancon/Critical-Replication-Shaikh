# Authorship and Register Guardrails

This note replaces the “AI-authorship stress test” language with a safer object:

> Register and over-smoothing audit.

The question is not whether text was AI-written. That is not a reliable diagnosis. The useful question is whether the prose has become too smooth, generic, uniform, or non-local relative to the chapter’s own intellectual register.

## Rename the tool

Use:

**Register / Over-Smoothing Stress Test — Ash / UMass / Diego Calibration**

Avoid:

**AI-authorship detector**

## What to flag

- uniform mid-length sentences;
- repeated paragraph shapes;
- generic transitions such as “moreover,” “furthermore,” “it is important to note”;
- bolted-on citations;
- repeated “this demonstrates” language;
- abstract claims that do not point to a table, figure, mechanism, or source;
- over-polished political-economy statements;
- econometric prose that has been beautified into vagueness.

## What not to flag

- dry results prose;
- technical repetition where clarity requires it;
- formulaic table descriptions;
- standard econometric terminology;
- authorial theoretical vocabulary when it is defined and load-bearing.

## Core rule

Do not manufacture imperfection. The goal is not to make prose awkward. The goal is to restore local specificity, uneven argumentative pressure, and authorial texture where the prose has become uniformly polished.

## Edit hierarchy

1. Correct grammar and syntax.
2. Preserve the claim.
3. Remove generic smoothing.
4. Keep technical prose dry when dry is better.
5. Allow political-economy interpretation only after the empirical claim is established.
