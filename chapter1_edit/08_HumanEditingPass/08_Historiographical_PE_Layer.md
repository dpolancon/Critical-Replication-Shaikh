# Historiographical Political-Economy Layer

Historical context is not background. It determines whether an econometric object is coherent.

This layer comes from the historiographical paper on the Chilean developmental state and the country-city transition. Its lesson for Chapter 1 is periodization discipline.

## Use this layer when

- defining regime windows;
- interpreting structural breaks;
- explaining why a dummy is not merely a statistical patch;
- connecting estimated coefficients to class conflict, state policy, accumulation, migration, labor discipline, or institutional change;
- distinguishing statistical stability from historical coherence.

## Do not use this layer to

- overstate causality;
- turn estimates into total historical explanation;
- replace econometric evidence with narrative confidence;
- make a results section sound like a political essay.

## Historical mechanism chain

1. World-market tendency: a change in the international division of labor, technical paradigm, trade regime, or external constraint alters the feasible space of national accumulation.
2. National regime formation: state, capital, and labor settle into a historically specific regime of accumulation and mode of regulation.
3. Social carrier mechanism: migration, unionization, urbanization, mechanization, state investment, labor discipline, or institutional incorporation stabilizes or destabilizes the regime.
4. Measured macroeconomic trace: the mechanism appears in observed series such as profit share, wage share, utilization, capital productivity, unemployment, investment, output, inflation, or sectoral composition.
5. Crisis or reproduction: the regime either reproduces itself through the mechanism or reaches a boundary where the same mechanism ceases to stabilize accumulation.

## Chapter 1 translation

A dummy year is not automatically a historical mechanism.

A historical break is credible only if the text answers:

- what changed historically;
- why that change affects the output-capital relation;
- whether the dummy captures an outlier, a level shift, or a regime boundary;
- whether the interpretation is supported by the model or belongs in a historical caveat.

## Model sentence

> The post-1974 window is not only a statistical subsample. It corresponds to a different historical organization of capacity formation, in which labor discipline, capital restructuring, and the changed composition of investment altered the conditions under which accumulation translated into productive capacity.
