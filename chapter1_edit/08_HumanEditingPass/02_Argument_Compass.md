# Chapter 1 Argument Compass

## The chapter in one sentence

Shaikh’s capacity-utilization index is approximately reproducible, but the output-capital coefficient that constructs the productive ceiling becomes theoretically meaningful only when treated as a historically and distributionally conditioned transformation elasticity.

## The chapter is not

- a general history of output gaps;
- a full theory of capitalist crisis;
- a complete model of distribution-led technical change;
- a technical appendix on ARDL/VECM methods;
- a declaration that Shaikh’s method is simply wrong.

## The chapter is

- a critical replication of Shaikh’s capacity-utilization measure;
- a reconstruction of the parameter that draws the productive-capacity path;
- a sensitivity analysis of the single-equation output-capital relation;
- a system-level test of whether that relation survives without distribution;
- a political-economy argument about why productive capacity cannot be treated as a neutral technical ceiling.

## Core claims

### Claim 1 — Reproducibility

A Shaikh-like capacity path can be approximately reconstructed from the available data and a plausible ARDL specification.

### Claim 2 — Parameter reinterpretation

The long-run output-capital coefficient is not only a fitted multiplier. It can be interpreted as a candidate transformation elasticity, theta, connecting capital accumulation to productive-capacity formation.

### Claim 3 — Single-equation non-uniqueness

The admissible ARDL space does not collapse onto one unique theta. The parameter is disciplined but not uniquely pinned down by single-equation evidence.

### Claim 4 — System non-self-sufficiency

The bivariate output-capital relation does not survive as a self-sufficient cointegrating system under the tested VECM design.

### Claim 5 — Distributional conditioning

The trivariate system suggests that distribution, proxied by logged exploitation, is part of the empirical condition under which the output-capital relation stabilizes.

### Claim 6 — Political-economy implication

Capacity utilization is not merely a technical residual. The productive ceiling is constructed through a relation that is conditional on accumulation, distribution, and historically specific shocks.

## Claims to demote or park

These may belong to later dissertation work, but they should not carry the conclusion of Chapter 1 unless directly estimated here:

- military spending stabilizes excess capacity;
- consumer debt stabilizes overaccumulation;
- theta is fully endogenous to demand;
- the chapter proves the full tendency of the rate of profit to fall;
- the empirical design establishes a complete theory of crisis.

## Prose rule

Use the word “shows” only when a table, figure, or test directly supports the statement.

Use “suggests” when moving from empirical result to interpretation.

Use “implies” only when the logical connection is explicit and not merely intuitive.

Use “proves” almost never.
