# Chapter 1 — Human Outline Proposal

## Working title

**Critical Replication of Shaikh’s Capacity Utilization Measure: From Reproducible Index to Conditional Capacity Parameter**

## Central movement

Shaikh’s index can be approximately reconstructed. However, the coefficient that makes the index possible is not just a technical scaling parameter. Once it is interpreted as a transformation elasticity between capital accumulation and productive capacity, its meaning depends on specification choice, historical controls, and the distributional structure of the system.

## 1. Introduction: what is being replicated and why it matters

The introduction should do only four things.

First, state the object: Shaikh constructs capacity utilization from a long-run relation between output and capital.

Second, state the replication question: can this relation be reproduced from the available data and methods?

Third, state the reinterpretation: the output-capital coefficient is not merely a statistical input; it can be read as a candidate transformation elasticity, theta, linking capital accumulation to productive-capacity formation.

Fourth, preview the finding: the Shaikh-like path is approximately recoverable, but theta is not unique in the single-equation space and is not self-sufficient in the system-level tests.

Do not fully explain ARDL, VECM, all dummies, all stages, and the full political-economy implication here. The introduction should invite the reader into the problem, not make them survive the whole chapter in miniature.

## 2. The measurement problem: capacity utilization and the hidden productive ceiling

This section should be the historical/conceptual setup, but shorter than the current version.

Its job is to explain why capacity utilization is hard to measure: observed output is visible, but productive capacity is not. Therefore, any utilization index depends on how the productive ceiling is constructed.

Keep the historical trace only where it clarifies the measurement problem:

- survey measures define capacity through reported normal operation;
- output-gap methods define capacity through macroeconomic filtering;
- political economy approaches ask what capacity means in relation to accumulation, profitability, and normal utilization;
- Shaikh’s method matters because it reconstructs the productive ceiling from an accounting relation rather than from surveys or policy filters.

This section should not become a general history of output-gap governance, Fordism, neoliberalism, or central banking. Those themes are relevant only if they clarify why the productive ceiling is theoretically underidentified.

## 3. From Shaikh’s coefficient to theta: the theoretical move

This is where the chapter should slow down.

The reader needs to understand the conceptual transformation:

Shaikh estimates a long-run output-capital relation to construct productive capacity. Your chapter asks what kind of parameter this coefficient is. If it governs how capital accumulation becomes productive capacity, then it can be interpreted as theta. If theta equals one, capacity grows proportionally with capital. If theta differs from one, accumulation and capacity formation are structurally unbalanced.

This section should define actual output, productive capacity, utilization, capital stock, theta, and balanced versus unbalanced capacity formation.

This is not yet the empirical results section. It is the conceptual hinge of the chapter.

## 4. Critical replication design: reproduction, sensitivity, and failure

This section should introduce S0, S1, and S2 as reader-friendly questions, not as a technical avalanche.

S0 asks: can a Shaikh-like capacity path be reconstructed from available corporate-sector data and reported dynamic choices?

S1 asks: if the single-equation setup is opened to reasonable ARDL alternatives, is the estimated theta unique or specification-sensitive?

S2 asks: if output and capital are placed in a joint system, does the relation survive without adding distribution and historical shock controls?

This section should also define the evidentiary vocabulary: reproducible, approximately reproducible, sensitive, admissible, non-unique, system-stable, and object-shifting alternative.

## 5. Stage S0: approximate reconstruction of Shaikh’s baseline

This is the first empirical results section.

Its job is to show that the baseline is not impossible to recover, but exact reproduction depends on data and lag choices that are not fully transparent.

The tone should be careful:

- not “Shaikh is wrong”;
- not “we prove fragility”;
- rather: “the published path is approximately recoverable, but the coefficient is sensitive to reasonable reconstruction choices.”

The key result is that relatively small changes in theta can generate meaningful shifts in the constructed utilization series.

## 6. Stage S1: the single-equation parameter is not unique

This section presents the ARDL grid.

The human claim is simple:

> Once the single-equation model is opened to reasonable lag structures, deterministic cases, information criteria, and historical controls, there is not one natural theta. There is an admissible range.

Discuss the 500-model grid, lag choices, deterministic specifications, information criteria, and why “best model” is not a purely mechanical category.

Keep the phrase “disciplined non-uniqueness” only if it is defined plainly: the parameter is not arbitrary, but it is also not uniquely pinned down by the single-equation evidence.

## 7. Stage S2: system evidence and the role of distribution

This section asks whether the output-capital relation survives when treated as part of a joint macroeconomic system.

The bivariate result should be stated soberly:

> The output-capital relation does not survive as a self-sufficient cointegrating system under the tested specifications.

The trivariate result:

> A restricted rank-one system survives when logged exploitation and historical shock controls enter the state vector.

Avoid saying “severe omitted variable bias” unless the econometric evidence directly supports that precise claim. A safer formulation is:

> The bivariate relation is not system-admissible under the tested design, while the trivariate system suggests that distribution is not an external interpretation added after estimation but part of the empirical conditions under which the relation stabilizes.

## 8. What has been learned: replication, theta, and capacity as political-economy object

This should be the synthesis section before the conclusion.

It should answer three questions:

1. What did the replication establish? Shaikh’s method is approximately reproducible.
2. What did the sensitivity analysis establish? The transformation parameter is not uniquely identified in the single-equation ARDL space.
3. What did the system analysis establish? The output-capital relation is not self-sufficient when tested as a joint system; it stabilizes only with distribution and historical controls.

Then the political-economy claim:

> Capacity utilization cannot be treated as a purely technical residual. The productive ceiling is constructed through a relation that is historically and distributionally conditioned.

## 9. Conclusion: what the chapter does and does not prove

The conclusion should be disciplined.

It can say:

- the Shaikh-like index is recoverable;
- theta is a useful reinterpretation of the output-capital coefficient;
- single-equation estimates generate a non-unique admissible parameter space;
- system evidence points to the importance of distribution and historical shocks;
- capacity identification in political economy requires more than a technical output-capital fit.

It should not state, as a final empirical finding, that military spending, consumer debt, or external demand components stabilize the system unless those variables are estimated in this chapter. Those can be future research or bridges to later dissertation chapters.
