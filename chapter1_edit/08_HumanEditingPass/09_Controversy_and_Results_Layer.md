# Controversy of Results Layer

Use this layer to handle disagreement without drama.

The Reinhart-Rogoff controversy and the Maziarz interpretation teach two complementary lessons.

HAP teaches that critical replication can identify errors, omissions, and inappropriate summary choices.

Maziarz warns that not every contrary result is a correction. Some contrary results arise from alternative but defensible methodological choices, changing data, different estimands, or publication incentives around refutation.

## Mistake vs. method rule

Do not call a result “wrong” until the source of divergence has been classified.

Use “error” for:

- miscoded formulas;
- transcription mistakes;
- missing observations caused by accident;
- non-reproducible calculations;
- inconsistent use of declared data.

Use “methodological choice” for:

- alternative weighting schemes;
- alternative sample windows;
- alternative admissible definitions of the variable;
- alternative estimators;
- alternative deterministic cases;
- alternative treatment of ambiguous observations.

Use “object shift” when:

- the capital boundary changes;
- the output concept changes;
- the price index changes;
- the periodization crosses a regime boundary;
- the method estimates short-run adjustment rather than long-run structure;
- the robustness check no longer estimates the same theoretical object.

## Chapter 1 use

When a result differs from Shaikh, classify the disagreement before interpreting it.

Ask:

1. Is this a computational reproduction issue?
2. Is this a data/source/vintage issue?
3. Is this a lag/deterministic-case issue?
4. Is this an admissible methodological alternative?
5. Is this an object shift?
6. Does the result overturn, weaken, bound, or merely reinterpret the original claim?

## Model sentence

> The divergence is not treated as a failed replication in the narrow computational sense. The alternative construction changes the capital boundary and therefore shifts the empirical object from capacity-building fixed capital to a broader asset aggregate. The exercise is informative as a robustness check, but it cannot be interpreted as estimating the same long-run capacity relation.
