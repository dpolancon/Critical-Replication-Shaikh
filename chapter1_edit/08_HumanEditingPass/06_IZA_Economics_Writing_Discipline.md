# IZA Economics-Writing Discipline

Use the IZA guidelines as editing constraints, not as a prose-style corpus.

## Use the 2021–2022 version for austerity

This version is the cleaner discipline filter:

- clarity over sophistication;
- active voice;
- direct sentences;
- formal citation integration;
- evidence-based claims;
- central novelty stated early;
- no unnecessary jargon;
- no vague “this”;
- no “clearly,” “obviously,” or “of course” unless the claim is genuinely trivial.

## Use the 2023–2024 version for paragraph architecture

This version is useful for structure:

- one idea per paragraph;
- opening sentence states the paragraph’s claim;
- following sentences prove, explain, qualify, or illustrate that claim;
- title, introduction, data, identification, results, tables, figures, conclusion, citations, and appendices each serve a visible function.

## Do not imitate the 2023–2024 voice

The 2023 guide sometimes sounds chatty. That is fine for a teaching document, but not for Chapter 1.

Use its rules, not its tone.

## Chapter 1 translation

Every section must reveal its job immediately.

Every technical paragraph should begin with the economic or econometric claim, then unpack the machinery.

Jargon is allowed only when load-bearing. ARDL, VECM, theta, GPIM, cointegration, admissibility, and productive capacity are load-bearing if defined. “Structural landscape,” “complex dynamics,” “framework,” and “pivotal” usually are not.

Results prose is allowed to be dry. Do not beautify tables into vague theoretical language.

Dry is not a bug. Dry is often the house style.
