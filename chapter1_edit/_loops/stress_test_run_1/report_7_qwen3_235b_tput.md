**Qualitative Verdict**

The chapter demonstrates strong command of the UMass heterodox political economy register in its conceptual core: the use of $\theta$, the framing of unbalanced growth, and the critique of technical closure all align with the department’s critical-replication tradition, particularly as exemplified in Herndon, Ash, and Pollin (2014). The integration of class struggle into the cointegration argument—especially via the rate of exploitation as a stabilizing variable—is rhetorically and theoretically at home in this corpus.

However, the **literature review and introduction** read as over-processed. The paragraph rhythm, connective density, and citation placement resemble a polished textbook or AI-assisted draft rather than the uneven, argument-driven prose of genuine UMass dissertations. The **stylized example** and **conclusion** suffer from excessive smoothness and uniform hedging, lacking the strategic roughness and boldness seen in Rebello or Ash.

Passages that would pass Ash’s scrutiny include the **VECM counterfactual analysis** and the **final synthesis on class struggle as econometric prerequisite**—these are conceptually sharp, citationally grounded, and rhetorically forceful. But the **ARDL grid description** and **output-gap critique** feel generic, relying on widely available econometric commentary rather than deep engagement with institutional specifics.

Revise toward **greater rhythmic irregularity**, **deeper citation integration**, and **strategic imperfection**—especially in early sections. The argument is strong; the voice needs to sound more like a UMass PhD candidate writing under pressure, not a perfectly tuned assistant.
