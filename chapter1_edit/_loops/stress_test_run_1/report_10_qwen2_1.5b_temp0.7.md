This parameter, reinterpreted as the transformation elasticity of productive capacity with respect to the capital stock ($\theta$), grounds Shaikh's empirical framework within an explicit macroeconomic closure, where $\theta$ represents the rate at which capital accumulation translates into productive capacity formation.

The analytical weight placed on $\theta$ introduces an unbalanced growth closure, where the long-run transformation elasticity deviates from unity ($\theta \neq 1$). This formulation breaks with the Harrodian knife-edge assumption of a unitary capacity transformation that underlies post-Keynesian and neoclassical models. Under an unbalanced growth closure, long-run disequilibrium emerges as a persistent structural feature of accumulation, rather than a temporary disturbance. The evaluation of stationarity becomes a test of whether aggregate demand buffers stabilize the expansion or whether capital accumulation follows a regime-dependent trajectory.

To execute this critical replication, we apply the autoregressive distributed lag (ARDL) model, carefully considering the bounds testing procedure to determine the presence of a long-run relationship between output and capital, and vector error correction model (VECM) testing to examine the dynamic interactions among variables. The ARDL model is a single-equation approach that estimates the long-run relationship between output and capital.
