# Section 3 — Compressed Pitch  
## From Shaikh’s Output–Capital Relation to the Transformation Elasticity

Actual output is observed. Productive capacity is not. Shaikh estimates productive capacity from a long-run relation between output and capital, then constructs capacity utilization as the deviation of observed output from that estimated benchmark. The central issue is therefore not only the utilization residual. It is the coefficient that generates the capacity path.

That coefficient is the focus of Section 3. Interpreted through the surplus approach to political economy, the long-run output–capital coefficient is not a generic elasticity of output with respect to capital. It is a candidate transformation elasticity, θ: the parameter that measures how capital accumulation becomes productive capacity under unbalanced growth.

The theoretical benchmark comes from reproduction. Smooth reproduction requires that output, capital, and productive capacity move together under strict proportional conditions. Balanced growth is the knife-edge case, θ = 1. Capital and productive capacity expand one-for-one. But capitalist accumulation need not satisfy that condition. Once θ differs from one, accumulation becomes structurally unbalanced: capital can expand faster than productive capacity, or productive capacity can expand faster than capital.

This gives Shaikh’s output–capital equation its theoretical force. His cointegrating relation estimates a capacity benchmark without relying on the Federal Reserve Board utilization series or on output-gap filters. That is the contribution: Shaikh shifts the problem from proxy choice to structural identification grounded in profitability accounting.

But the same strategy has a bound. Shaikh’s single-equation ARDL specification recovers one coefficient for the full sample. Once that coefficient is interpreted as θ, this fixity becomes a substantive claim. It assumes that the transformation of accumulation into productive capacity is stable across the historical period being estimated.

Section 3 therefore prepares the critical replication. The empirical question is not simply whether Shaikh’s utilization path can be redrawn. The question is whether θ is robustly identified: stable, unique, admissible, and able to survive when the relation is tested as a system rather than only as a single equation.

Section 4 follows from that problem. Stage S0 asks whether the single-equation benchmark can be approximately reconstructed. Stage S1 asks whether the admissible ARDL space yields a unique θ. Stage S2 asks whether the relation survives as a bivariate output–capital system, and whether it survives only when exploitation is included explicitly in a restricted trivariate system.

The chapter’s intervention is therefore precise: Shaikh opens the structural route; the critical replication tests whether the coefficient recovered by that route can bear the theoretical weight placed on it.
