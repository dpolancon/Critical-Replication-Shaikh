> This chapter reinterprets Shaikh's methodology by shifting the focus from the error-correction residuals—which proxy capacity utilization—to the estimated cointegrating coefficient itself. We interpret this parameter as a capacity transformation elasticity, $\theta$, which measures the percentage change in productive capacity generated by a one percent change in the capital stock. Standard post-Keynesian models typically assume a balanced-growth closure by constraining this elasticity to unity ($\theta = 1$). Under this restriction, Harrodian instability operates purely as a behavioral process: firms adjust investment in response to deviations of actual demand from normal capacity utilization. If $\theta \neq 1$, however, capacity formation is structurally decoupled from capital accumulation. An elasticity below unity ($\theta < 1$) characterizes an overaccumulation regime where capital growth outpaces capacity expansion, whereas an elasticity above unity ($\theta > 1$) leads to a regime of chronic excess capacity. By separating the transformation elasticity $\theta$ from Harrodian dynamics, we investigate whether this parameter remains stable across alternative single-equation specifications and whether the bivariate relationship between output and capital holds in a joint system.
